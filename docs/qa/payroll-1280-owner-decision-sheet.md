### F-01 — Where do final wages belong?

**Question:** When someone leaves halfway through a period, will the remaining wages be paid once in ordinary payroll or only in a reviewed final-pay package?

**Current code on main:** [src/lib/approved-exit-payroll.ts:16-52](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/lib/approved-exit-payroll.ts#L16-L52) conditionally admits approved separating workers to ordinary payroll and rejects missing/double approvals, conflicting last days and any approved final-pay unpaid-basic amount before ordinary payroll. [src/app/api/separation/route.ts:194-217](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/app/api/separation/route.ts#L194-L217) refuses a crossing released cutoff without an exact approved-exit trace; [src/app/api/separation/route.ts:576-585](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/app/api/separation/route.ts#L576-L585) rejects a second unpaid-basic claim for the traced capped period and requires final statutory review for unpaid wages. These are useful guards, but the approved-exit ordinary-pay route still exists behind an OFF-by-default gate. The current implementation does **not** establish a universal single final-pay-only wage ledger for earlier unpaid periods or same-date independent packages.

**Verified governing sources / operative text:**
- [P.D. No. 851 (1975), IRR §2(a)–(b)](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1975/pd_851_1975.html) defines “one twelfth (1/12) of the basic salary” and how earnings are classified. [DOLE Labor Advisory No. 21, s. 2026, §IV](https://dole.gov.ph/php_assets/uploads/2026/11/LA-21-26-Guidelines-on-the-Payment-of-Thirteenth-Month-Pay.pdf) confirms separation-period prorating. [BIR RR No. 11-2018, §7, amended §2.79, annualized withholding method](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/local/pdf/RR%20No.%2011-2018.pdf) covers termination-year tax settlement.
- **SOURCE NOT VERIFIED:** A statute commanding the platform's *exclusive* ordinary-versus-final wage posting design, the source-month attribution of unrelated late wages, or how to consolidate several packages dated the same day. Treat them as **unproven**, not as permission to pay them twice.

**Code agrees? DISAGREES with preferred final-pay-only ownership, although existing blockers agree with no-duplication safety.** Fictional worker has a ₱24,000.00 monthly base and a hypothetical 15-day half-period, works 7 eligible days: ₱12,000.00 × 7 / 15 = **₱5,600.00** earned. Under the proposed method the ordinary unpaid component is **₱0.00** and a single independently reviewed final-pay wage line is **₱5,600.00**. Paying that line again as ordinary wages yields **₱11,200.00** and a **₱5,600.00** duplicate. This is an illustration of double posting, *not* a new statutory divisor rule.

**Exact choice to sign:** “For our one-site standard pilot, every still-unpaid wage through an employee's approved last workday is assigned to one signed final-pay package, not a second ordinary payroll payment. We retain existing separation authorization, crossing-period, end-date, non-overlap, statutory-review and missing-evidence holds. Unrelated prior-period wages and more than one package on the same date are BLOCKED pending separate source-matched evidence and finance/legal approval. We will not activate the alternate approved-exit ordinary-wages path for this pilot.” No finding closed.

### F-05 — Is an unworked regular holiday payable?

**Question:** What must the employee prove about the preceding workday to receive a regular-holiday benefit while not working the holiday?

**Current code:** [src/lib/payroll-engine.ts:1551-1596](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/lib/payroll-engine.ts#L1551-L1596) loads evidence of a preceding scheduled workday with valid in/out punches or approved paid leave. For non-monthly pay, [src/lib/payroll-engine.ts:3038-3090](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/lib/payroll-engine.ts#L3038-L3090) adds 1× daily pay for an eligible unworked regular holiday. If evidence is missing, it adds no wage and inserts an exception *flag*. That path does not itself demonstrate a hard stop on release; a flag is not proof of a release blocker. Other category-specific exemptions must be evaluated before enabling this pilot scenario.

**Verified source and operative text:** [Labor Code Article 94; Omnibus Rules Implementing the Labor Code (1989), Book III, Rule IV, §6(a), (c)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/2/85819). §6(a) says employees “on leave of absence with pay” are entitled to the holiday benefit, and defines the unpaid-preholiday exclusion. §6(c) extends the preceding-day test backwards across a scheduled rest/non-working day. No presumption of eligibility may be fabricated from absent attendance.

**Code agrees? DISAGREES only as to required *hard blocking*; computation evidence matches the verified normal rule.** Fictional covered daily worker: base **₱684.25**. Proved work on the last scheduled day before the regular holiday, then unworked holiday => holiday entitlement **₱684.25**. If the scheduled predecessor was a rest day, examine the working day before that instead. Missing punch/leave proof yields **₱0.00 provisional posting** and **₱684.25 unresolved**, *not* a legal conclusion that the worker is ineligible or can be released unpaid. The engine presently flags and omits; strict release prevention is not established by these lines.

**Exact choice:** “For eligible covered pilot workers, apply Rule IV §6 using documented work attendance or paid leave on the relevant preceding scheduled workday, including rest-day lookback. If the required evidence or covered employment classification is missing, hold that worker and the impacted payroll release as an unresolved exception; never quietly convert missing proof into a zero entitlement. Any employer policy exceeding the statutory minimum needs attached evidence and separate approval.”

### F-08 — What belongs in 13th-month salary, and what if the balance is negative?

**Question:** How do we build the exact annual basic salary, especially for piece-rate workers, commissions, paid leave and separation?

**Current code:** [src/lib/final-pay.ts:31-104](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/lib/final-pay.ts#L31-L104) accumulates signed **BASIC**, attributed same-tax-year **RETRO**, eligible leave and reductions for LATE/UT; it does **not** universally classify separately coded piece-rate pay, qualifying sales commissions, policy-integrated bonuses or maternity differential as basic. [src/lib/thirteenth-month-centavos.ts:1-12](https://github.com/erwinmehehe/payrollph/blob/328267e5abfce557e614bf77396ba867e1b8f8b3/src/lib/thirteenth-month-centavos.ts#L1-L12) has a half-up integer-centavo option but its activation is **not approved**. **Verified sources / operative text:**
- [P.D. 851 (1975) as modified by Memorandum Order 28 (1986), and IRR §2(a)–(b)](https://lawphil.net/statutes/presdecs/pd1975/pd_851_1975.html): “one twelfth (1/12) of the basic salary” of the calendar year; earnings included for services, exclusions depend on actual integration into basic pay.
- [DOLE Labor Advisory **No. 21, s. 2026**, §§I–IV](https://dole.gov.ph/php_assets/uploads/2026/11/LA-21-26-Guidelines-on-the-Payment-of-Thirteenth-Month-Pay.pdf): “total basic salary earned” / 12, covers piece-rate, guaranteed-plus-commission, multiple-employer and separated employees, and a maternity-leave worker receiving salary differential. Unpaid absences reduce what was **earned**. Ordinary unintegrated OT, holiday, night premium, leave conversion and allowances are **excluded**; those integrated by agreement, policy or established practice are **included**. Piece-rate output earnings form their base. A separation gets its proportionate annual earned base, not an extra full month's entitlement. The advisory text's introductory “P.D. No. 581” is inconsistent with the **verified actual P.D. 851**; do not treat that editorial citation as a different statutory basis.
- [Philippine Duplicators, Inc. v. NLRC, G.R. **110068**, **11 Nov 1993**, holding](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1993/nov1993/gr_110068_1993.html): “sales commissions form part of the wage or salary”; the Court held fixed salary **plus integral sales commissions** belongs in that case's 13th-month base. The 2026 DOLE advisory at §III both refers to “total earnings” for fixed-plus-commission and gives a *fixed-only* worked example citing **Boie-Takeda, G.R. 92174 and 102552 (10 Dec 1993)**. **SOURCE AMBIGUOUS:** do not blanket-include every commission, or blanket-exclude genuine basic remuneration. Require category facts/contract and practitioner classification before invoking the particular holding.
- [Labor Code Art. 113, and DOLE Department Order **195, s. 2018** amending Book III, Rule VIII, §10(b)](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/10/91259): a wage deduction for payment to employer/third person must meet the **written authorization** and **no employer pecuniary benefit** conditions. No automatic recovery based on a negative calculator amount.
- **SOURCE NOT VERIFIED:** an automatic statutory deduction right to recover overpaid 13th month, a single universal maternity salary-differential accounting classification (the advisory verifies **coverage**, not every employer-specific payroll-line treatment), and the particular integration of unproved employer benefit schemes.

**Code agrees? DISAGREES for eligible separately coded earnings and negative-balance visibility; AGREES for ordinary known-basic/12 and nonnegative disbursement.** Fictional worker under a documented integral sales commission scheme has **₱60,006.00** basic fixed salary earned and **₱6,006.00** qualifying integral commission, while **₱2,400.00** discretionary *non-integrated* incentive is excluded. Eligible base = **₱66,012.00**, 13th = **₱5,501.00**. BASIC-only coding gives ₱60,006.00 / 12 = **₱5,000.50**, under by **₱500.50**. For a separate piece-rate worker with ₱18,003.00 documented eligible output wages, 13th = **₱1,500.25**. For a separated worker whose eligible earned basic was ₱13,200.00, entitlement is ₱1,100.00; if ₱1,225.00 already paid, **current due is clamped ₱0.00** and hypothetical **₱125.00** excess needs a **visible *disputed* receivable review**, *not* a worker-debt determination or recovery instruction. These are independent examples.

**Exact choice:** “For each **individual legal employer**, compute its own calendar-year **total basic salary actually earned / 12**, subtract this same employer's prior 13th payment, and pay **max(0, balance)**. Respect earned pay reduced by unpaid absences, lateness and undertime; pro-rate separated employees. Do not pool 13th entitlement between concurrent or prior employers (BIR prior-employer evidence remains a *tax* matter). Include policy-proven integrated basic benefits and qualifying integral sales commissions per the cited ruling; include piece-rate output earnings; exclude unintegrated OT, holiday/premium, ND, cash-converted unused leave and discretionary allowances. Fixed-plus-commission classification, multiple-employer evidence and maternity salary-differential treatment require case-specific payroll/legal verification before enabling those nonstandard cases. A negative owed balance pays **₱0.00** but creates an auditable **possible receivable / human-decision flag**, not a presumed legally collectible debt; **never deduct automatically**. Do not silently suppress category gaps, negative balances or prior-year attribution uncertainty.”
